Research programme

Numerical methods for coupled physics in fractured media.

My work is centred on reliable discretisations for systems where mechanical deformation, fluid flow, heat transfer, and contact/friction interact across complex fracture networks.

Research architecture

Four levels of the problem

This structure makes the scientific story easier to follow: start from the physics, then the mixed-dimensional geometry, then the contact law, then the nonlinear solver.

01 · physics

THM and poromechanics

Coupled pressure, temperature, and displacement fields in fractured porous media.

02 · geometry

Mixed-dimensional fractures

Fractures/faults represented as lower-dimensional interfaces embedded in the matrix.

03 · constraints

Contact and friction

No-penetration, normal compliance, Coulomb friction, stick/slip regimes, and fracture aperture.

04 · computation

Robust nonlinear solves

Fixed-point coupling, Newton–Krylov acceleration, Newton–Raphson, and semismooth Newton contact solves.

Physics applications

Where the methods are used

Geothermal stimulation and fault reactivation

Fluid injection can modify pressure, stress, and frictional contact states in fracture networks. The numerical challenge is to resolve flow-mechanics coupling while preserving stability and handling contact transitions.

CO2 geological storage and induced seismicity

Mixed-dimensional THM models help assess pressure and temperature effects in fractured geological storage systems, including fault reactivation and leakage/seismicity risk.

Fractured porous media and reservoir-scale computation

Hybrid finite volumes, VEM, and Nitsche methods are well suited for meshes with tips, corners, intersections, and nonmatching geometric features.

Corium-concrete interaction in nuclear safety

Current postdoctoral work focuses on multiphysics code coupling between thermal-hydraulics and thermomechanics for severe-accident simulations, including ablation, source terms, and cracking.
