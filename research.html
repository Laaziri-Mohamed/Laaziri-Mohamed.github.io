Research

I specialise in numerical methods for partial differential equations and coupled physical systems. My current application is nuclear severe-accident modelling; my doctoral work focused on thermo-hydro-mechanical processes in fractured porous media.

Current research: nuclear severe accidents

At CEA IRESNE, I work on corium–concrete interaction: the coupled processes that occur when molten reactor materials come into contact with concrete during a severe accident.

My research develops numerical models for heat transfer, concrete ablation, and the evolution of the interacting materials. Ongoing work towards CIMAC–CAST3M coupling aims to connect the thermal-hydraulic calculation with the mechanical response of concrete. This is a model-development contribution to understanding accident phenomena and improving simulation methods.

Applications of the fractured-media models

Fluid injection and geothermal systems

Changes in fluid pressure and temperature can alter stresses in a fractured rock and trigger fracture opening or sliding. Coupled models provide a way to investigate these interactions in numerical experiments, including injection-driven fault reactivation.

Geological storage

Similar couplings matter when studying the response of fractured formations to fluid injection or thermal loading. The numerical methods provide building blocks for such simulations; their application to a particular site also requires appropriate material laws, geological data, and validation.

My contribution

I develop and analyse methods that handle fracture geometry, contact constraints, and coupling between physical fields. Computational benchmarks assess their accuracy and behaviour under controlled model assumptions.
