Postdoctoral Researcher · CEA IRESNE

Mohamed Laaziri

Applied mathematician working on multiphysics modelling, numerical analysis, and scientific computing for thermo-hydro-mechanical systems in fractured media and nuclear-safety applications.

I develop and analyze numerical methods for coupled mechanics, flow, and heat transfer: finite elements, finite volumes, Virtual Element Methods, Nitsche formulations, mixed schemes, and frictional contact models. My current postdoctoral work focuses on multiphysics code coupling for corium-concrete interaction and severe-accident modelling.

Research focus

Numerical methods for coupled physics

My work sits at the interface of applied mathematics, computational mechanics, geosciences, and nuclear safety. I focus on schemes that are robust on complex fractured geometries and consistent with the energy structure of the physical model.

THM modelling

Fractured porous media

Mixed-dimensional models for thermo-hydro-mechanical processes with matrix-fracture coupling, poroelasticity, Darcy flow, and heat transfer.

Contact mechanics

Nitsche and mixed formulations

Weak enforcement of contact constraints and Coulomb friction at fracture interfaces, including VEM and Lagrange-multiplier strategies.

Nuclear safety

Corium-concrete interaction

Multiphysics coupling between thermal-hydraulics and thermomechanics to improve predictions of concrete ablation, cracking, and degassing.

Current position

CEA IRESNE, Cadarache

Postdoctoral work on code coupling for severe-accident simulations, with numerical exchanges between CIMAC and CAST3M in the ANR IMMOC project.

CIMAC - CAST3M coupling

Development of numerical exchanges for heat fluxes, geometry evolution, ablated concrete source terms, and degassing terms between a multiphase thermal-hydraulics code and a finite-element thermomechanics code.
