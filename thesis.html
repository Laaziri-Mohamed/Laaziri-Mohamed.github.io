Numerical modelling of thermo-hydro-mechanical processes in fractured porous media

PhD in Applied Mathematics · Université Côte d’Azur · 2025

Read my PhD thesis on HAL — version 1

This is the version I recommend reading and sharing: tel-05472966v1.

Overview

My thesis develops and analyses numerical methods for thermo-hydro-mechanical processes in fractured porous media. These models couple fluid flow, heat transfer, and rock deformation, while accounting for contact and friction along fractures.

The central challenge is to represent these coupled processes on meshes adapted to complex fracture geometry, while retaining mathematical stability and reliable numerical behaviour.

Main topics

  • Mixed-dimensional models of fractured porous media.
  • Mixed and Nitsche discretisations of contact and friction.
  • Virtual Element Methods on polytopal meshes.
  • Discretisations preserving energy estimates for coupled thermal, hydraulic, and mechanical processes.
  • Nonlinear solvers and computational evaluation of the proposed methods.

Applications

The models investigate how pressure and temperature changes affect deformation and fracture opening or sliding. These questions are relevant to geothermal fluid injection and geological storage. The thesis contributes numerical methods and their analysis, evaluated through computational test cases.

This doctoral research provides the numerical-methods background for my current work on nuclear severe accidents.
